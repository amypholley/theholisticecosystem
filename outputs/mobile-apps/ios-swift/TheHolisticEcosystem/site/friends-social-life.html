Friends & Social Life

Friendships and social connection can provide belonging, enjoyment, perspective, practical help, and emotional support. A healthy social life can include a few close relationships, a wider community, online connection, activity-based friendships, or a mix that fits the person.

Why Friendships Matter

Connection and Belonging

Friendships can provide companionship, shared identity, emotional support, and a place to be known.

Support Systems

Friends may provide practical, emotional, informational, recreational, or emergency support.

Quality vs. Quantity

A large network is not required. Reliability, safety, mutuality, and fit often matter more than the number of contacts.

Social Wellness

Social wellness includes connection, boundaries, participation, support, communication, and enough solitude.

Building and Maintaining Friendships

Making New Friends

Use repeated activities, classes, volunteering, hobbies, faith communities, groups, apps, or introductions.

Maintaining Friendships

Reach out, remember important details, make plans, show appreciation, and adapt through busy seasons.

Group Activities

Choose environments that match interests, sensory needs, cost, transportation, and preferred group size.

Friendship Boundaries

Discuss availability, confidentiality, money, favors, emotional capacity, digital contact, and expectations.

Supportive Friends

Supportive friends respect limits, celebrate growth, communicate honestly, and allow mutual care.

Navigating Social Challenges

Social Confidence

Prepare conversation starters, attend for a limited time, bring support, and focus on curiosity rather than performance.

Social Anxiety

Use gradual steps and qualified support when anxiety significantly restricts life.

Friendship Conflict

Address concerns directly, listen, repair when possible, and recognize when distance is healthier.

Loneliness and Isolation

Start with one regular point of contact and use low-pressure or accessible forms of connection.

Changing Friendships

Some friendships change with distance, values, health, family, work, or life stage; grief and gratitude can coexist.
