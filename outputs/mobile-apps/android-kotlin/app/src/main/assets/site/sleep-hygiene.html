Sleep Hygiene

Sleep hygiene includes flexible habits and surroundings that can support better sleep. The goal is not a perfect routine, but a set of cues that helps your body and mind prepare for rest.

Understanding the Topic

Sleep hygiene includes bedtime and morning routines, bedroom comfort, light exposure, screen management, food and caffeine timing, and calming evening habits.

Common Challenges

Inconsistent Schedules

Work, school, caregiving, symptoms, or changing energy can make regular sleep times difficult.

Overstimulation

Screens, noise, racing thoughts, sensory discomfort, or busy evenings can make winding down harder.

Uncomfortable Environment

Light, temperature, pain, shared rooms, or limited resources may affect rest.

Practical Strategies

Easy

Dim one light, silence one notification, prepare tomorrow's essentials, or use the same short wind-down cue each night.

Moderate

Create a repeatable evening routine, adjust the bedroom environment, and build a steady morning light routine.

Advanced

Track patterns, coordinate light, caffeine, activity, and routines, and build a complete sleep-support system around your schedule.

Special Considerations

Shift workers may need blackout curtains and a protected daytime sleep window. Parents and caregivers can use shorter routines. Neurodivergent people may benefit from sensory-friendly bedding, predictable cues, or gradual screen transitions. Chronic pain, illness, disability, and limited budgets may require adaptable routines using what is already available.
