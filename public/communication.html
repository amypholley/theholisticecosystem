Communication

Communication helps people share information, express emotions and needs, solve problems, build trust, and repair misunderstandings. Strong communication is a learnable skill, and different people may communicate through speech, writing, assistive technology, sign language, behavior, or other methods.

Why Communication Matters

Relationships

Clear, respectful communication supports understanding, trust, intimacy, and repair.

Career and Teamwork

Communication helps people coordinate responsibilities, share information, prevent errors, and collaborate.

Leadership

Healthy leaders explain expectations, listen, invite feedback, and address concerns directly.

Self-Advocacy

Communication helps people request support, state preferences, disclose selectively, and protect boundaries.

Core Communication Skills

Verbal Communication

Use specific language, appropriate tone, useful examples, and enough context for the listener.

Nonverbal Communication

Facial expression, posture, eye contact, silence, distance, and tone may affect meaning, but they vary across cultures and neurotypes.

Active Listening

Give attention, ask clarifying questions, summarize what you heard, and check rather than assume.

Assertive Communication

State needs, limits, and opinions clearly without aggression, intimidation, or unnecessary apology.

Emotional Honesty

Share feelings accurately and responsibly without making another person responsible for regulating every emotion.

Asking for Needs

Make requests specific, realistic, and open to discussion while respecting the other person's right to respond.

Difficult Conversations

Prepare

Clarify the issue, desired outcome, timing, safety, and what you can control.

Use Specific Examples

Describe observable behavior and impact rather than attacking character or motives.

Stay Curious

Ask how the other person experienced the situation and listen for information you may have missed.

Take Breaks

Pause when overwhelmed and agree on when to return rather than abandoning the conversation indefinitely.

Repair Attempts

Acknowledge impact, apologize specifically, clarify intent without erasing harm, and describe what will change.

Common Communication Mistakes

Mind Reading

Assuming motives or feelings can create conflict. Ask directly when it is safe.

Listening to Respond

Preparing a rebuttal while someone speaks makes important information easier to miss.

Global Statements

Words such as always and never can turn one issue into an attack on the whole person.

Indirect Requests

Hints may be missed. Clear requests reduce confusion.

Avoiding Repair

Ignoring hurt can allow resentment and misunderstanding to grow.

Building Better Communication Habits

Regular Check-Ins

Discuss needs and concerns before they become emergencies.

Reflect Before Reacting

Notice emotion, assumptions, and goals before responding.

Choose the Format

Some conversations work better in writing, by phone, with visual supports, or with a neutral third party.

Practice and Feedback

Ask trusted people what helps them understand you and what communication habits create difficulty.
