Fiber

What Is Fiber?

Fiber is a type of carbohydrate that the body does not fully digest. Unlike many carbohydrates that are broken down for energy, fiber moves through the digestive system largely intact and plays an important role in overall health.

Daily Fiber Recommendations

Women

Approximately 25 grams per day

Men

Approximately 38 grams per day

Individual needs may vary based on age, activity level, medical conditions, and dietary preferences.

Types of Fiber

Soluble Fiber

Dissolves in water and forms a gel-like substance.

Common Sources: Oats, beans, apples, citrus fruits.

Insoluble Fiber

Adds bulk and supports digestive health.

Common Sources: Whole grains, vegetables, nuts, seeds.

Many foods contain a combination of both soluble and insoluble fiber.

Benefits of Fiber

Digestive Health

Fiber helps add structure to the diet and supports regular digestion.

Fullness & Satiety

Fiber-rich foods can help meals feel more filling and satisfying.

Heart Health

Fiber-rich eating patterns can be part of a heart-supportive lifestyle.

Blood Sugar Support

Fiber can slow digestion and may help support steadier energy after meals.
