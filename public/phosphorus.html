Phosphorus

This page is for education only and does not diagnose or treat a deficiency. Symptoms can have many causes, and laboratory testing may be needed to confirm a nutrient problem.

What Is This Nutrient?

Phosphorus is a major mineral and electrolyte. The body needs it to carry out essential processes, and an appropriate intake supports normal health without replacing individualized medical care.

Primary Functions

Building bones and teeth

Phosphorus contributes to this process as part of a balanced pattern of nutrients and healthy body function.

Producing and storing cellular energy

Phosphorus contributes to this process as part of a balanced pattern of nutrients and healthy body function.

Forming cell membranes and DNA

Phosphorus contributes to this process as part of a balanced pattern of nutrients and healthy body function.

Foods Rich In Phosphorus

Excellent Sources

  • Yogurt
  • Milk
  • Salmon

Good Sources

  • Chicken
  • Lentils
  • Cashews

Additional Sources

  • Potatoes
  • Whole grains
  • Processed foods with phosphate additives

Food amounts vary by variety, soil, fortification, brand, preparation, and portion size.

Ways To Support Healthy Levels

A varied diet usually provides enough. People with kidney disease should follow individualized advice about phosphorus and additives.

A varied eating pattern is usually the best foundation. Supplements can be useful in specific situations, but the right form and dose depend on age, diet, health conditions, medications, pregnancy, and laboratory results.

Possible Signs Of Deficiency

Possible signs may include:

  • Weakness or poor appetite
  • Bone pain or fragile bones
  • Confusion in severe deficiency

These symptoms can have many causes and do not confirm a deficiency. Consider discussing persistent or concerning changes with a healthcare professional.

Possible Risk Factors For Deficiency

  • Severe malnutrition or refeeding
  • Alcohol use disorder
  • Certain antacid use
  • Rare genetic or kidney-related disorders

Restricted diets, digestive disorders, aging, medical conditions, and certain medications can change nutrient needs or absorption. Individual assessment matters.

Possible Signs Or Risks Of Excess Intake

  • High blood phosphorus is especially concerning in kidney disease
  • Large amounts of phosphate additives may contribute more readily than natural food phosphorus

Food and supplements are not interchangeable sources of risk. Keep supplements away from children and follow product labels and professional advice.

When To Speak With A Healthcare Professional

Persistent Symptoms

Discuss ongoing fatigue, weakness, pain, digestive changes, or other symptoms rather than assuming they come from one nutrient.

Neurological Or Heart Symptoms

Promptly seek care for new numbness, severe weakness, confusion, balance changes, fainting, or heart-rhythm concerns.

Supplement Questions

Ask about the appropriate dose, medication interactions, medical conditions, pregnancy, and whether laboratory testing is useful.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can food provide enough?

Often, yes, but needs and absorption vary. Fortified foods or supplements may be appropriate for some diets, life stages, and medical situations.

Should I take a supplement for symptoms?

Symptoms alone cannot identify a deficiency. A healthcare professional can help evaluate causes, testing, and a safe plan.

Can more be harmful?

Yes. Some nutrients interact with medicines or become harmful at high doses. Avoid assuming that a larger dose is better.
