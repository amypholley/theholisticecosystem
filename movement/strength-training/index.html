Strength Training Resources

Strength training does not have to mean heavy lifting or intimidating gyms. It can begin with a wall, a chair, a resistance band, light weights, machines, water exercise, or everyday movements.

Why This Matters

Strength supports daily tasks like standing, carrying groceries, climbing stairs, posture, joint stability, and independence. It can also help people feel more capable in their own bodies.

Practical Ideas

Bodyweight Basics

Try sit-to-stands, wall push-ups, step-ups, or gentle squats.

Use Simple Tools

Resistance bands, soup cans, light dumbbells, or machines can all work.

Train Daily Life

Practice lifting, carrying, reaching, pushing, and pulling safely.

Rest Matters

Give muscles time to recover between harder strength sessions.

Safety & Pacing Notes

Move slowly, breathe normally, and start lighter than you think you need. Stop if you feel sharp pain or unsafe symptoms. A trainer, physical therapist, or clinician can help if you need modifications.

Try This This Week

  • Pick two simple strength moves.
  • Do 1-2 easy sets twice this week.
  • Focus on control, not perfection.
