Adaptive Exercise Resources

Adaptive exercise starts with the truth that every body deserves access to movement. Activities can be adjusted for disability, chronic illness, injury, energy level, sensory needs, mobility aids, or where you are today.

Why This Matters

Adapted movement can support strength, circulation, flexibility, mood, independence, and confidence. It also makes movement more welcoming because the goal is not to copy someone else; the goal is to find what works for your body.

Practical Ideas

Seated Movement

Try seated stretching, chair cardio, arm circles, or gentle marching.

Use Support

Use a wall, chair, resistance band, mobility aid, or water support.

Short Sessions

Break movement into 2-5 minute sessions throughout the day.

Modify Freely

Change speed, range of motion, position, equipment, or rest time.

Safety & Pacing Notes

Adaptive movement should feel supportive, not punishing. Pause for sharp pain, dizziness, unusual symptoms, or overwhelming fatigue. Professional guidance from a physical therapist or medical provider can help with complex needs.

Try This This Week

  • Choose one seated or supported movement.
  • Practice it for 2-5 minutes twice this week.
  • Notice what adaptation made it easier.
