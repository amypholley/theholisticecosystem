Significant Others

Healthy romantic relationships are built through consent, respect, communication, trust, emotional safety, compatible expectations, and ongoing care. Relationships can take many forms, and no relationship status determines a person's worth.

Building Healthy Romantic Relationships

Communication

Discuss needs, expectations, affection, responsibilities, conflict, finances, intimacy, and future plans openly.

Trust & Commitment

Trust grows through honesty, consistency, appropriate privacy, fidelity agreements, and accountability.

Emotional Intimacy

Share feelings and experiences gradually while respecting boundaries and different communication styles.

Physical Intimacy

Physical intimacy requires ongoing consent, comfort, communication, respect, and freedom to change one's mind.

Partnership and Growth

Shared Goals

Discuss values, finances, living arrangements, family, work, health, lifestyle, and long-term priorities.

Relationship Needs

Partners may need different amounts of closeness, solitude, affection, reassurance, novelty, routine, or social time.

Maintaining the Relationship

Use regular check-ins, appreciation, shared experiences, practical teamwork, affection, and repair.

Growing Together

Support individual development while revisiting agreements as people and circumstances change.

Healthy Independence

Maintain personal identity, friendships, interests, privacy, and appropriate decision-making autonomy.

Safety and Support

Healthy Conflict

Disagreement should not involve intimidation, threats, coercion, stalking, humiliation, or violence.

Seeking Support

Relationship education or qualified counseling may help when both people can participate safely.

Abuse Concerns

When abuse is present, prioritize confidential safety planning and specialist support rather than couples counseling.
