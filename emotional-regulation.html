Emotional Regulation

Emotional regulation is the ability to move through emotions with awareness and choice. It does not mean staying calm all the time or forcing feelings away. Regulation helps lower overwhelming intensity, understand what an emotion may need, and choose a response that supports safety, values, and long-term well-being.

Notice

Pause long enough to recognize the emotion and what may have activated it.

  • Name the feeling
  • Notice body sensations
  • Identify the trigger
  • Rate the current intensity

Settle

Use a manageable tool to help the nervous system move toward a steadier state.

  • Slow the breath
  • Ground through the senses
  • Reduce stimulation
  • Use supportive movement or rest

Respond

Choose an action after the emotion becomes easier to understand and carry.

  • Communicate a need
  • Set a boundary
  • Take one practical step
  • Ask for support when needed

Regulation Is Personal

A strategy that helps one person may not help another. Sensory needs, trauma, neurodivergence, chronic illness, fatigue, and the situation itself can all affect what feels regulating. Build a small collection of options rather than expecting one technique to work every time.
