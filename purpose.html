Purpose Wellness

Purpose provides direction, meaning, and motivation. While purpose may evolve throughout life, having a sense of purpose can help guide decisions, increase resilience, strengthen well-being, and create a greater sense of fulfillment.

Purpose is not limited to a career, productivity, parenthood, achievement, or one permanent calling. It can be found in relationships, recovery, learning, faith, creativity, service, community, and daily care.

Discovering Your Purpose

Understanding Purpose

Purpose is an evolving sense of direction, significance, and contribution rather than one predetermined answer.

Purpose vs. Career

Work can express purpose, but purpose can also come from relationships, care, faith, learning, creativity, or community.

Purpose vs. Goals

Purpose provides direction; goals are selected outcomes that may express that direction.

Different Life Stages

Purpose may change through education, work, relationships, illness, caregiving, retirement, grief, or reinvention.

Exploring Interests

Follow recurring curiosity and try small experiences without demanding immediate certainty.

Identifying Strengths

Notice abilities, character, lived wisdom, skills, and ways others seek your help.

Recognizing Passions

Pay attention to topics, problems, activities, and people that consistently matter.

What Matters Most

Use values, relationships, needs, faith, and the impact you hope to have as guides.

Purpose & Well-Being

Mental Health

Purpose may support help-seeking, coping, routine, and reasons to continue, but it is not a substitute for treatment.

Emotional Wellness

Meaningful activity and connection may support fulfillment, emotional processing, and identity.

Resilience

Purpose can help people orient toward what remains important during setbacks and uncertainty.

Motivation

A personally meaningful reason can support action when novelty or enthusiasm fades.

Adversity

During hardship, purpose may become rest, survival, accepting care, protecting someone, or taking one day at a time.

Major Life Changes

Transitions can disrupt old purpose and create space for gradual rebuilding.

Strengths & Contribution

Identifying Strengths

Review accomplishments, feedback, coping, interests, roles, and tasks that feel natural or meaningful.

Natural Talents

Talents are starting points that still benefit from support, practice, and opportunity.

Personal Gifts

Patience, humor, compassion, observation, persistence, creativity, and lived experience can all contribute.

Helping Others

Ask what support is wanted and contribute without ignoring consent, boundaries, or your own well-being.

Service and Contribution

Volunteer, mentor, advocate, create, care, teach, solve problems, or participate in community.

Positive Impact

Impact can be small, local, quiet, indirect, and still deeply meaningful.

Personal Mission Statement

What Is It?

A personal mission statement summarizes the values, direction, people, and contribution you want your life to reflect.

Why Create One?

It can help compare opportunities, set boundaries, choose goals, and remember priorities during change.

Questions to Consider

Who do I want to be? What matters? Whom do I serve? What strengths do I use? What impact do I seek?

Drafting

Write one or two clear sentences using language that feels natural rather than impressive.

Revising Over Time

Review the statement after major changes and update it as understanding develops.

Example Template

I aim to use [strengths] to support [people or purpose] through [actions] while living by [values].

Purpose Through Life Stages

Young Adulthood

Explore identity, relationships, education, work, values, community, and possibilities without requiring a final answer.

Career Development Years

Balance growth, financial needs, relationships, health, contribution, and work that fits.

Family & Relationship Years

Purpose may include care, partnership, chosen family, community, boundaries, and shared goals.

Midlife Reflection

Reassess inherited expectations, achievements, losses, values, health, and the years ahead.

Retirement & Reinvention

Build purpose through relationships, learning, service, creativity, mentorship, rest, and new routines.

Major Transitions

Illness, loss, relocation, separation, disability, caregiving, and career change may require redefining what purpose looks like.

Purpose Action Plan

Step 1: Identify Values

Choose the principles and priorities you most want life to reflect.

Step 2: Identify Strengths

List skills, character, experience, knowledge, and support available.

Step 3: Explore Interests

Research, experiment, volunteer, create, learn, and talk with others.

Step 4: Clarify Goals

Choose meaningful outcomes and realistic next steps.

Step 5: Create Meaningful Habits

Build repeatable actions that express values and support goals.

Step 6: Contribute to Others

Use sustainable service, care, creativity, or participation.

Step 7: Review and Adjust

Reflect regularly and revise purpose as life and understanding change.
